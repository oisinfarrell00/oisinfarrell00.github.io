# Introduction

Dan Brown has a specific talent, and it is a real one: he makes it almost physically impossible to put the book down.

# Pure Momentum

Robert Langdon, a Harvard professor of symbology, is called to the Louvre in Paris after the museum's curator is found murdered, having left behind a series of cryptic clues. What follows is a breathless race across Europe, weaving through secret societies, art history, and the hidden corners of religious tradition.

Brown's technique is relentless. Short chapters, a cliffhanger at the end of almost every one, vivid and specific settings — Paris, London, the Scottish countryside — all working together to keep you moving forward at pace. The book grabs you almost from the opening page and barely loosens its grip. Whether you find the religious history genuinely fascinating or just enjoy the plot machine regardless, it works either way.

The user of puzzles is very interesting as well in that you are more inclined to follow along as you the reader want to figure out the secret to the book. With regards to the symbols, puzzles and cryptic messages I was very impressed with how they all tied together.

It is not a subtle book, and it is not pretending to be. What it is, as pure momentum-driven storytelling, is hard to fault. Exactly what a thriller should be.

# Rating

9
