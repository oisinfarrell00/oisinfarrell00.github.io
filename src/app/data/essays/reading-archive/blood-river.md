# Introduction

Tim Butcher's Blood River earns a spot as one of the most interesting non-fiction books that I have ever read. The book follows the author, Tim Butcher—a journalist for The Daily Telegraph—on his wild attempt at retracing H.M. Stanley's famous expedition through the Congo, traveling inland to the Congo River and then sailing down it.

The book reveals not only an epic adventure but also a continent forgotten by the rest of the world.

# Bravery or Naivety

One thing is for sure: Tim Butcher has a serious amount of bravery or is a very silly fellow altogether. He was based in South Africa and references that his mother spent a lot of time in Africa. Thus he knows the danger he is getting himself into, yet he takes on the epic journey. Along the way he faces many challenges. A lot of the situations he finds himself in are extremely dangerous.

# A Glimpse at Africa

Prior to this book I had little to no knowledge about Africa. I was aware that it was currently underdeveloped, but I was not aware that it was once a bustling colonial hub with industry and opportunity. This is starkly contrasted with the Africa in the book, where Tim Butcher describes a barren land void of any opportunity where crime and danger run rampant. I knew that corruption and lawlessness were bad, but I didn't know to what extent. Of course, not all of Africa is like this, and along his journey he stays in a range of accommodations from literal mud huts to functioning hotels in the old colonial towns.

# Africa Forgotten

One thing I have come to appreciate (or feel for) is how Africa was used for its resources then forgotten as soon as they dried up. It is a sad reality for much of the continent.

# Final Note

This book is a very cool book that offers a lot.

# Rating

8.5
