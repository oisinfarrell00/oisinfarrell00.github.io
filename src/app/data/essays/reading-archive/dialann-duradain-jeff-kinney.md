# Introduction

I did not come to this book as a straightforward reader. I came to it as a language learner. Im currently working on my Irish by listening to Raidió na Gaeltachta, working through a grammar book, and picking up what I can from podcasts. I was looking for a way to make reading in Irish feel like something other than homework.

# An Unusual Solution

For me the choice of book was very important. If the vocabulary is too advanced or the subject matter too dry, the exercise would become more frustrating than useful and inevitablly I would give up. This led me to _Dialann Dúradáin_, the Irish translation of _Diary of a Wimpy Kid: Rodrick Rules_. My brother originally gave me this idea when he was learning spannish and read Diario de Greg, the Spannish version of the book. I had already read the English original as a child and with the plot already in my head, I could focus on the language without getting lost in the story. The Irish used is accessible for a learner — suitably simple without being patronising — and the cartoon-heavy format means you are never drowning in text.

More importantly, it is genuinely enjoyable. The humour translates well, and there is something quietly satisfying about finding yourself laughing at a joke in a language you are still learning to trust. It works.

# A Very Personal Note

One caveat — and any other reader can disregard this entirely — the dialect used was not quite the Donegal Irish I had hoped to reinforce, given that my partner is a native speaker from Donegal. Some of the vocabulary and constructions were not ones she would use. This is an extremely specific, extremely personal complaint and has absolutely no bearing on the book's quality for anyone else. As a language learning tool and an enjoyable read, it does the job very well.
