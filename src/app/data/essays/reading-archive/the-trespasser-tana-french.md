# Introduction

Tana French is a writer with obvious talent, and _The Trespasser_ has genuine things going for it. It is just that those genuine qualities are outweighed, in the end, by structural problems that are difficult to overlook.

# What Works

Set in Dublin — which was a refreshing change of scene — the novel follows Detective Antoinette Conway, a woman navigating a predominantly hostile male environment within the Dublin Murder Squad. The city is rendered with real authenticity; this does not feel like a generic thriller backdrop dressed up in an Irish coat. The Dublin of _The Trespasser_ feels lived-in and specific.

Conway herself is also a more interesting character than I initially expected. The tough, emotionally armoured female detective is a type I often find tiresome — when done badly it can feel like a checklist rather than a person. French handles it with more care than most, grounding Conway's armour in something that feels believable rather than performed.

# Where It Struggles

The difficulty is the plot itself. For the vast majority of the book, the story appears to be heading quite clearly in one direction. When the pivot eventually comes, the reveal feels forced rather than earned — less a satisfying surprise than an authorial decision to swerve away from where the material was naturally pointing. The sense is of a twist being reached for rather than built toward. In a genre where the reveal is everything, that is a significant problem.

There is also a very cliche sub plot in the book that seems to come and go in a flash and seems to have almost no relevance other than to build the character. To me this felt very forced and I really didnt like it.

The raw material is genuinely here. The writing ability is good. It just does not come together the way it should.

# Rating

4
