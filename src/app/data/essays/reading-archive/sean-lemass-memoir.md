# Introduction

The Lost Memoirs of Seán Lemass is based on a series of candid interviews conducted by businessman Dermot Ryan in the late 1960s. The book condenses 22 hours of tape-recorded conversations into a single volume, offering a rare, unfiltered look into the mind of the man often cited as the "Ireland's Greatest Politician".

# Summary

Lemass’s pragmatic view of the world is his defining characteristic. To rule and make decisions based on fact rather than emotion is a fascinating quality. Often referred to as the "Architect of Modern Ireland," his economic brilliance steered the nation away from the failed policy of "protectionism" toward a globalized future.

His foresight, particularly regarding the aviation industry (founding Aer Lingus) and attracting Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), changed the trajectory of the State. Perhaps his greatest strength was his lack of ego; he was a leader who actively sought brilliance in others, most notably in his partnership with the legendary civil servant T.K. Whitaker.

He was a man of immense personal integrity. Despite decades in power, he was famously incorruptible and ended his political career without wealth. Following his retirement, he transitioned into the private sector, serving as Chairman of Erin Foods and the Irish Sugar Company, as well as taking on several board directorships. He even famously refused a ministerial pension for several years because he felt his director fees in the private sector were enough to live on. Lemass proved that one could serve their country with passion and then turn their efforts to supporting their family with the same honest work ethic. He died a man of modest means but immeasurable legacy.

# The Rebel Years

Lemass’s journey began not in a boardroom, but in the fire of the 1916 Easter Rising. At just 16 years old, he fought in the GPO. During the War of Independence, he was a member of the "Twelve Apostles"—Michael Collins’s elite intelligence unit.

The "ugly" reality of his rebel years came during the Civil War. Lemass was staunchly Anti-Treaty and fought in the Four Courts. The conflict was deeply personal; his brother, Noel Lemass, was abducted and murdered by Free State agents. Despite this trauma, Seán Lemass became the first major Republican leader to realize that shooting your way to a Republic was a dead end. This realisation led him to join Éamon de Valera in breaking away from Sinn Féin to found Fianna Fáil in 1926, choosing to play the game of democracy to achieve his goals.

# The Political Years

Lemass spent the majority of his career as Minister for Industry and Commerce, where he became the engine room of the government.

State-Building: He was responsible for creating the infrastructure of the modern state, establishing semi-state bodies like ESB, Bord na Móna, and Aer Lingus. He believed that if the private market wouldn't build the necessary industries, the State must.

The 1958 Pivot: By the late 50s, Ireland was stagnant and dying from emigration. Alongside T.K. Whitaker, Lemass authored the First Programme for Economic Expansion. He did the unthinkable for a nationalist: he opened the doors to foreign companies and began the process of joining the European Economic Community (now the EU).

The Northern Bridge: As Taoiseach (1959–1966), he broke a 40-year cold war by traveling to Belfast in 1965 to meet Northern Prime Minister Terence O'Neill. It was a move of pure pragmatism, prioritizing trade and North-South cooperation over symbolic rhetoric.
