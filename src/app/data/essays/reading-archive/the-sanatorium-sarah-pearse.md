# Introduction

_The Sanatorium_ had everything it needed to be a great thriller. It had the setup, the atmosphere, the isolation. What it did not have was a satisfying ending and in this genre, that is a problem that undoes almost everything that came before it.

# A Promising Start

An old psychiatric institution converted into a luxury hotel high in the Swiss Alps. A snowstorm descends. Guests are cut off from the outside world. A killer is among them. On paper, this is a thriller writer's dream, and for a significant portion of the book, Pearse makes good on that premise. The setting is genuinely eerie, the sense of claustrophobic dread is well constructed, and the early chapters build tension with real skill.

# Where It Falls Apart

The problem is the reveal. Without going into specifics, the ending is a serious disappointment. So much so that my brother abandoned the book before reaching it, which with hindsight may have been the wiser call. Having committed to finishing it myself, I can confirm: the payoff does not match the build-up, and the gap between the two is not a small one.

There is also a subplot built around a character overcoming a past trauma at a critical moment — a trope that can work when handled with care, but which here felt too telegraphed to land with any real force. It just does not stick the landing.

# Rating

2
