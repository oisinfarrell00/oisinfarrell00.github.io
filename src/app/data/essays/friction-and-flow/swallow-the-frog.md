# Introduction

Years ago, I was a master procrastinator. If I had important work to avoid, you could find me doomscrolling, meticulously cleaning my room for the third time, or staring into the fridge for the millionth time hoping new food had magically appeared.

There is a deeper psychology to work avoidance that is well above my pay grade, but I reckon three major factors are at play.

First, humans are hardwired to avoid unnecessary exertion. This likely stems from our evolutionary past when life was brutal and energy was a scarce resource to be conserved. Second, we have built a modern world where comfort is aggressively available. We crave comfort, and society has delivered it on demand: YouTube, Netflix, endless scrolling, and instant food delivery.

Yet, this hyper-comfortable environment hasn't made us happier. Dare I say it, it’s made things worse in the mental department.

---

# Swallow the Frog

I won't pretend I invented the phrase "swallow the frog" — that credit goes to motivational public speaker Brian Tracy. Simply knowing the terminology won’t automatically cure procrastination, but the premise is simple: do your hardest, most friction-heavy task first thing in the morning.

I try to implement this concept every day, but with a specific twist.

One of my biggest hurdles in the past was starting my day by checking my phone. I would scroll through social media, consuming easy, unearned dopamine before my feet even hit the floor. You don't need a degree in neuroscience to realize that frying your attention span first thing in the morning sets you up for failure. I knew I needed a hard reset.

---

## What is My Frog?

I want to be clear: the last thing I want is to sound like a "bro-science" caricature. But my frog happens to be cold showers.

For the last three years, I have taken a cold shower every single morning. And let me tell you — even after three years, I still hate it. It is deeply uncomfortable, and every single morning I would prefer to stay in bed. But I’ve held this streak for too long to quit now.

Instead of opening social media—in fact, I’ve deleted it entirely (I know this is a cringey thing to say but it's true and this is my website so there you go. Also, if you must know, I have youtube but I only watch long form videos). Instead I step into cold show. It completely flips the script on how my day starts. Later on, when a difficult or tedious task lands on my desk at work, a thought naturally creeps in: _“Well, the worst part of my day is already over. This is objectively easier.”_

Choosing discomfort first thing in the morning trains my brain to just get on with it.

---

## Find Your Frog

What works for me might not work for you. You might honestly think I’m a bit mad, and that’s fair. Almost no one I talk to takes solace in a freezing shower; to most, it sounds like literal hell.

But the specific task doesn't matter. There is a friction task for everyone — you just have to find yours. If you can start your day by intentionally leaning into a bit of productive friction rather than running toward unearned comfort, you set your momentum for everything that follows.
